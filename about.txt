Agriculture is one of the major industries supporting food security worldwide, employment and economic growth, especially in the areas where agricultural cultures are usually the major source of income. In a lot of third world countries, the rural economies have remained predominantly agricultural, providing food and labor to millions of families. Timely availability of information, systematic planning of production, transparent trade transactions in the markets and ensured logistics systems are necessitating to provide efficiency in movement of farm products to consumer. These factors alleviate post harvest losses, reduce the spoilage of food and weaken the monetary expenses of the producers and consumers and ensure that the quality and supply of food remain intact.  
	In the Philippine setting, small scale farmers are faced with a set of structural and operational challenges that limit the productivity and income potential. These problems include disintegrated farm-management behavior, limited entry to formal market, poor communication of transportation and delivery infrastructure and a strong dependency on middlemen or mediators. The result is that farmers are often forced to receive unfavorable prices of their harvest, and their bargaining power is lowered, and the losses directly after the harvest are high. Lack of direct market connectivity and low logistical aid worsens agri-business inefficiency even further.  
	In addition, without an end-to-end and unified digital solution, farmers cannot efficiently track inventory, plan harvest, oversee deliveries, and establish the face-to-face communication with buyers. At the same time, the consumers and local agricultural authorities are faced with the challenge of getting proper and timely data that relates to the volumes of production, trends of prices, and supply levels. Such inadequacy of transparency and traceability limits the capacity of stakeholders to make sound decisions, effect fair price processes and to come up with responsive agricultural policies and interventions.  
	To address such issues, the current paper will offer a centralized farm-to-market trading and logistics infrastructure that will combine the processes of inventory control and monitoring, harvesting and delivery times, monitoring prices, registering transactions, and reporting all in one online platform. The system is supposed to work with the supervision of the municipal agricultural authorities in order to enhance accountability and transparency and reliability of the data.  
	The paper at hand will assess the planned system based on how it manages to streamline its operations, enhance the transparency of the market, and strengthen data-driven decision-making with respect to the idea of price fairness, the reduction of crop losses, and the sustainability of the Philippine agricultural practices in the long run.
